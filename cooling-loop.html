Project Overview

The goal of this project was to design an experimental cold plate cooling test loop to see how gyroidal cold plates would improve heat dispersion and affect pressure in a liquid cooling system. The loop had to fit within the benchtop area the lab provided while still letting the cold plates be tested under controlled, repeatable conditions.

I picked out the components based on the project's requirements, including the pumps and the types of tubing, then optimized where every component sat on the bench. Finally, I modeled the entire system in SolidWorks and put together a Bill of Materials with costs, purchase links, and the use of each part.

What the Loop Measures

The loop was designed around two questions: how effectively the gyroid lets the water carry heat away, and how much pressure the gyroid costs compared to a conventional cold plate.

Measurement 01
Heat Removal

Cartridge heaters plugged into a copper block generate the heat load, and the block is connected directly to the cold plate. Thermocouples sit on either side of the cold plate, still within the test section, and measure the temperature difference of the water flowing through it. That difference shows how effectively the gyroid lets the water displace the heat.

Measurement 02
Pressure Drop

A gyroid redirects the flow much more than a cold plate with straight slits, which creates more turbulence. Two pressure transducers, one on each side of the test section, measure how much pressure is lost across the plate. The transducers read pressure without letting any fluid flow through them, so they don't restrict the flow they are measuring.

Design Process

Every placement decision in the loop traded off against pressure loss and pump performance, so the layout was treated as part of the engineering problem rather than an afterthought.

01
Component Selection

Selected the pumps, the types of tubing, and the rest of the loop hardware against the requirements of the experiment, so the loop could compare cold plate designs under controlled conditions.

02
Layout Optimization
Positioned every component within the provided benchtop area to:
  • Minimize total tube length
  • Minimize the number of bends in the tubing
  • Use gravity to help the pump and maximize its efficiency
03
Full-System CAD

Built the complete loop as a single SolidWorks assembly of more than 200 components. Tubing was modeled by sweeping profiles along 3D sketch paths routed through the system, so the model reflects real tube runs and bend locations.

04
Procurement BOM

Produced a Bill of Materials listing each part with its use, unit price, quantity, and purchase link, with formulas totaling the cost. Total purchase cost: $1,261.74. Everything else the loop needed was already owned by the lab.

My role: I owned the design phase, from requirements through the final assembly and BOM. I wasn't able to see the loop get built, so this page covers the design and what it was built to measure rather than test results.
