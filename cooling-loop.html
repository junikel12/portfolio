Project Overview

The goal of this project was to design an experimental cold plate cooling test loop to see how gyroidal cold plates would improve heat dispersion and affect pressure in a liquid cooling system. The loop had to fit within the benchtop area the lab provided while still letting the cold plates be tested under controlled, repeatable conditions.

I picked out the components based on the project's requirements, including the pumps and the types of tubing, then optimized where every component sat on the bench. Finally, I modeled the entire system in SolidWorks and put together a Bill of Materials with costs, purchase links, and a justification for each part.

Design Process

Every placement decision in the loop traded off against pressure loss and pump performance, so the layout was treated as part of the engineering problem rather than an afterthought.

01
Component Selection

Selected pumps, tubing types, and the rest of the loop hardware against the requirements of the experiment, so the loop could deliver the flow conditions needed to compare cold plate designs.

02
Layout Optimization
Positioned every component within the provided benchtop area to:
  • Minimize total tube length
  • Minimize the number of bends in the tubing
  • Use gravity to help the pump and maximize its efficiency
03
Full-System CAD

Built the complete loop as a single SolidWorks assembly of more than 200 components. Tubing was modeled by sweeping profiles along 3D sketch paths routed through the system, so the model reflects real tube runs and bend locations.

04
Procurement BOM

Produced a Bill of Materials listing every part with its cost, a purchase link, and a written justification for why it was chosen, ready for the lab to order from.

My role: this project covers the design phase, which I owned from requirements through the final assembly and BOM.
